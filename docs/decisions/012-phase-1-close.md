# 012: Phase 1 closes on accuracy; the speed gate is missed by 23 times

Date: 2026-11-09

## Context

Phase 1 asked whether the PGD energy subproblem can be solved accurately and quickly with real data,
with no multigroup library. Its gate, for a homogenized PWR mix (UO2, water, Zr at 600 K) and an
SFR mix (MOX, sodium, steel at 900 K), each against an OpenMC twin with the same data and settings:

1. k-infinity within 50 pcm.
2. Nuclide reaction rates within 0.2%.
3. A fine-mesh spectrum consistent with the tallies' statistics.
4. Particle balance closing in every run.
5. A full-range fixed-source solve within 10 s on one core at about 500k points, a placeholder to
   revisit in Phase 4.

The final runs are on study 04's grid (all reactions thinned to 0.1%, subdivide 2), with the code
at this commit; their logs are in `studies/07-phase-close/results/`, gitignored, regenerated by
the commands in [study 03](../../studies/03-k-infinity/README.md) and
[study 05](../../studies/05-unresolved-range/README.md).

## Decision

Close Phase 1 on items 1 to 4, which every case meets, and leave item 5 to you: it is missed by
a factor of 23, and closing it needs a change of method or of language, not tuning.

| Gate | SFR, tables off | SFR, tables on (64 bands) | PWR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Twin | `sfr.json`, 1e8 histories | `sfr-ptables.json`, 1e8 | `pwr.json`, 2e8 |
| 1. k - twin | +11.1 pcm (twin 6.9) | +16.5 pcm (twin 6.6) | +12.7 pcm (twin 6.7) |
| 2. Largest gated rate | Pu240 capture +0.015% (1.0 sd) | Pu240 capture +0.073% (5.0 sd) | U238 nu-fission -0.015% (1.5 sd) |
| 2. Largest of any rate | Cr52 capture +0.060% (1.4 sd) | Cr52 capture +0.091% (2.0 sd) | O16 capture +0.067% (1.2 sd) |
| 3. Flux bins, rms z | 0.97 (257 bins) | 1.19 (257) | 1.02 (566) |
| 4. Balance residual / source | -1.2e-4 | -1.9e-4 | -3.9e-5 |
| 5. Fixed-source solve | 456 s, 4 sweeps | 527 s, 4 sweeps | 231 s, 6 sweeps |
| Nodes | 837,234 | 837,326 | 508,754 |

Gated rates are those whose twin uncertainty is a fifth of the gate or less (0.04%): every fission
and nu-fission rate, actinide capture and H1 capture. Structural and coolant capture is reported,
not gated. The balance residual is the truncation error of the panel quadrature; it falls by four
per subdivision (studies 02 and 03) and meets decision 005's 1e-4 from subdivide 4. Every flux bin
of every case lies within 3.8 sd; the PWR's lowest bins sit high (+15% at 5.2e-5 eV, 3.0 sd)
because the grid's bottom at 1e-5 eV cuts off upscatter from below.

**Speed.** On study 03's SFR mix at a 1% grid (244,110 nodes), one sweep takes 45 s, of which 43 s
is the anisotropic elastic correction, about 14 microseconds per node per nuclide above a few
hundred eV, whatever the nuclide's number of tables. Without it a sweep takes 1.6 s, and without
inelastic rows as well 0.5 s. So:

- At 500k nodes, a sweep without anisotropy would take about 3.4 s, and the four to six sweeps a
  solve needs about 14 to 20 s, still above 10 s.
- M9 removed the one waste it found: round-off upward deposits in four nuclides' inelastic rows
  (1e-11 of a row) started the thermal block's GMRES on the SFR mix, which halved its speed.
  Solves are now 2.7 times faster there; no result changed.
- Thinning angle tables saved nothing at 1e-3 and a fifth of a sweep at 3e-3, for 1e-3 of the
  flux, so it stays off; the cost is per node, not per cosine piece.

The ways to the target, for you to choose between:

- **Legendre compression of the centre-of-mass tables.** mu_cm is linear in E / E', so a table
  expanded to order L makes the window a polynomial in E / E', held in O(L) running sums per node
  however many tables the window crosses. This removes the anisotropic cost, at a truncation
  error to measure per nuclide, and leaves about 14 to 20 s.
- **Krylov acceleration of every lagged sweep,** as the thermal block already has. Solves take 4 to
  6 sweeps, and 14 to 17 with orders above zero; GMRES on the lagged hat emission would cut those.
- **The C++/Kokkos port the plan assigns to Phase 4,** with batching over terms, where the march's
  per-node work is a flat loop the GPU can take.

## Consequences

Phase 2 inherits the energy subproblem as `zerog.energy.subproblem.solve_energy`, with the same
signature since M1: effective densities per term for removal and each scattering order, a
streaming coefficient and a source, returning F(u) and, with probability tables, the shielded
cross sections. `zerog.energy.mix.build_mix` assembles a mix; `zerog.energy.eigen.solve_k`
iterates it; `zerog.linalg` is the Krylov interface. And these limits:

- **Physics left at order 0.** Inelastic, thermal and probability-table scattering have no
  Legendre orders above zero (study 06), and probability tables are shielded at order 0 only.
- **Thermal data.** Only incoherent inelastic S(alpha, beta) (H in H2O) is supported; coherent and
  incoherent elastic tables refuse. Hydrogen is free gas to 4e4 kT and at rest above (decision
  009). The PWR's +10 pcm floor and +0.13% thermal-peak bias are unexplained (study 03).
- **Probability tables.** In a heterogeneous problem OpenMC keeps a flight's r across surfaces and
  materials, which a shared in-leakage source drops (decision 011). The PWR mix with tables on
  was not run.
- **Grids and temperatures.** The grid is valid at the temperatures it was thinned at, one per
  mix; nothing between tabulated temperatures was run (decision 010).
- **Speed.** 231 s for a 500k-node fixed-source PWR solve, as above. Kernel builds take 6 to 7
  minutes per grid on 32 processes, and kernel caches are opt-in (`ZEROG_KERNEL_CACHE`).
- **Kernel form.** The march, band and moment kernels allocate inside loops and build lists, which
  the GPU port will need to flatten (reviews of M7 and M8).
